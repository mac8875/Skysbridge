How Skysbridge works

How to create a memorial star for your child

Skysbridge was created so that every child can have a place that remains. Creating a memorial takes only a few minutes. You can keep each star private or request public display, and change this choice any time in your account. Public details appear only after moderator approval.

Learn more about child loss, remembrance and memorials →

Before you begin

Create your account in three steps.

1

Select “Create account”

Go to Community and select Sign in to request access. In the window that opens, choose Create account, then enter your email address and a password.

2

Confirm your email address

Wait for the confirmation email from Skysbridge. Open it and click or tap the confirmation link. If it does not arrive, please check your spam or junk folder.

3

Sign in to Skysbridge

Return to Skysbridge, select Sign in, and enter the same email address and password. You can then enter the member area and create a memorial.

Then: four simple steps

Create a place that feels true to your child.

1

Begin with their name

Tell us your child’s name and, if you wish, something of their story. Dates, country and a photo are optional. You can upload one JPEG, PNG or WebP image up to 5 MB. There is no need to share more than feels right.

2

Choose and change their star’s visibility

You can keep the star private or request public display, and change this setting any time in your account. When the star is private, its name remains on the sky while the remembrance and other details stay private. A public request goes to moderation; details appear only after approval. Approved photos are visible only to signed-in members.

3

Special days remain special

On your child's birthday, their star shines a little brighter. On the anniversary of their passing, a candle appears in remembrance.

4

Return whenever you want

Your child's place can be visited and shared with the people you choose. You may also ask us to correct or delete a memorial by contacting Skysbridge.

Privacy and dignity

Your child’s story remains in your hands.

Privacy is the starting point, never an afterthought. Nothing becomes public without a clear request. There are no likes, rankings or advertising around a child’s memory—only the dignity of the child and the wishes of the family.
